CAMERA OBSCURA: DIAGRAM AND SOURCE NOTES

DIAGRAM DESIGN

Delivered CAMERA_320.PNG is an original 320 x 200 indexed PNG with a 4-bit
palette, using black and white only. It is designed for a Tandy-friendly
16-color display. CAMERA_640_MONO.PNG is an optional 640 x 200 one-bit
version, horizontally doubled with nearest-neighbor sampling. Both were
visually inspected at their native dimensions. The source script is
draw_camera_diagram.py.

An upright arrow represents the object. Its inverted image lies on the rear
screen. The opaque front wall has a gap centered at (155, 100). The object
endpoints are (36, 63) and (36, 137); the corresponding image points are
(274, 137) and (274, 63). Two sample rays cross exactly at the pinhole and
continue straight. Small chevrons show travel from left to right. No lens or
mirror is shown.

Labels identify OBJECT, DARK BOX, WALL, PINHOLE, SCREEN, UPRIGHT, and
INVERTED. All text uses hard-edged bitmap lettering. The PINHOLE leader is a
short horizontal line; the two long diagonal lines are light rays.

Caption: Light from the top reaches the bottom. Light from the bottom
reaches the top. The rays continue straight through the pinhole.

Only two sample rays are shown. The actual image is formed by light from
points all over the scene.

SOURCES CHECKED OCTOBER 7, 2026

1. University of Kentucky, The Physics of Light: Images.

https://www.pa.uky.edu/sciworks/courses/light/image4a.htm

Supports straight-ray geometry, inversion, a lensless camera, and the
brightness/sharpness tradeoff. Used for the explanation and diagram.

2. Science World, Camera Obscura.

https://www.scienceworld.ca/resource/camera-obscura/

Supports a closed cardboard box, foil aperture, clean small pinhole, sealed
seams, and a bright external scene. Their white interior screen differs from
our translucent rear screen.

3. University of Wisconsin-Madison, Make a Pinhole Camera.

https://wonders.physics.wisc.edu/make-a-pinhole-camera/

Supports a translucent paper screen, shielding of the viewing side, and
observing an inverted live image. Our shoebox layout is an original
adaptation. The article deliberately excludes solar viewing.

4. MIT, Foundations of Computer Vision, Lenses.

https://visionbook.mit.edu/lenses.html

Supports why a large aperture admits overlapping bundles, while a small
aperture gives a dim image. The aperture sorts directions; it does not focus
light by bending rays like a lens.

5. J. Paul Getty Museum, Devices of Wonder, Dark Chambers, page 6.

https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/devices/flash/pdf/p_themes_in_depth.pdf

Supports room-size and portable camera obscuras, use as drawing aids and
entertainment, and moving color images before photography. No disputed
inventor or priority claims are made in the article.

6. Rego et al., Deep Camera Obscura, author research paper.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05563

Supports optical diffraction and low light throughput as limitations of
lensless pinhole imaging.

EDITORIAL CHECKS

Article: 844 words including title and section headings. Original prose.
ASCII only, hard-wrapped at 76 columns, with CRLF line endings. No
photographic recording, lens, chemicals, mains electricity, or direct solar
observation is required.

Safety: adult sharp-tool caution; keep the Sun out of the field and never
aim at it or look through the pinhole at it. The viewing cloth has breathing
space and must not cover the front aperture. No hot lamps or flames are
proposed.

Viewing geometry: subject faces the foil end; viewer stays behind the
opposite translucent screen and looks at the screen itself. This avoids the
common construction error of using opaque paper while trying to view it from
behind. A cloth shields the viewer, screen, and intervening space from
ambient light. This is a source-grounded design, not a claim of physical
prototype testing.

Scope of historical claims is deliberately modest. The article discusses
inversion vertically, avoiding confusing left/right descriptions that depend
on which side of the screen is viewed. The pinhole's suggested
half-millimeter size is a practical starting point, not an asserted
universal optimum.

Revision: The opening now introduces the series premise, defines a camera
obscura before the experiment, and explains why it is the first installment.
The science, experiment, and diagrams are unchanged.
